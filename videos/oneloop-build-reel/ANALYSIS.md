# Analysis: the skit's story, and what the three style references do

## The skit (WhatsApp video, 26.3 s, 478×850, 24 fps): read frame by frame
Three shots (cuts measured at 8.79 s and 15.00 s):
1. **The demo** (0–8.8 s, wide two-shot at a cafe table). The developer (yellow check shirt, glasses) presents the software on the
   laptop; the client (light-blue check shirt, beard) watches, hand on chin.
   - Developer, 1.3 s: "That's about the entire software." Then a second line at 3.1–4.4 s that is unintelligible.
   - Client, 5.3 s: "The demo looks fine." 6.8 s: **"So you can deploy it."** (the go-ahead; v1's "But you can't deploy it" was a mis-hearing)
2. **The crash** (8.8–15.0 s, new angle: time has passed). The client turns on the developer, gesturing.
   - Client, 9.0–10.9 s: a complaint that is unintelligible ("hundreds of …"). 11.1 s: "Please do something!"
   - Developer, 13.2 s: "I'll do something." The client turns back to the laptop.
3. **The call** (15.0–26.3 s). The client walks off, dials, and phones the manager:
   "Hey, hi. Listen, the demo is actually good. But I don't know what's the problem, but it failed in deployment. Can you…"
   (1.4 s pause: he turns and looks back at the developer) "…just change the developer, please?" That pause is the joke, so it stays.

**Transcription check:** Whisper medium and turbo, on the raw and the cleaned audio, with and without a context prompt, plus focused decodes
of the three doubtful spans. Every line above is what the models agree on, except one word: medium hears "But **I** don't know", turbo "But **you** (don't) know"; the caption follows medium, which heard it the same way on the raw take and on the final mix. The two unintelligible lines got a different text from every
decode (e.g. "It won't be a body of work" / "In this moment we have bought it" / "and it's moving to your body"), so they're cut rather
than captioned with a guess. Word onsets around the long pause were read off the speech envelope, where Whisper's timings drift.

**Edit rule (Tarun's note: "keep it natural, no heavy cuts"):** the shots play in order. The only cuts remove dead air and the two
unintelligible lines; each one lands on a change of framing (wide → closer on the speaker), or on the "3 weeks later" card, so none reads
as a jump.

# What the three style references do, and what this reel takes from each

All three references are screen recordings of Instagram Reels (measured from frames at 2 fps, Whisper word timings and audio).

## Ref 1: podcast clip compilation (418×688, 27 s)
- **Format:** two podcast guests, hard-cut every 2–4 s, with movie-scene cutaways (a man in despair, a couple, a crying woman) playing under the same caption track.
- **Captions** (the signature):
  - two tiers: small lead-in words above, then one **big bold lowercase word** in a heavy sans (white);
  - the next word fades in grey before going white, so the eye is pulled word by word;
  - sits at about 65 % of the frame height, never on faces.
- **Editing:** the B-roll cutaways land on emotional keywords ("crisis", "despair", "saviour", "love"), illustrating each one. No transitions, just hard cuts on the beat.
- **Taken:** a heavy white sans in word-by-word reveal, keywords promoted (we colour them copper), and a cutaway on each keyword (the "real world" b-roll and motion-graphics cards).

## Ref 2: solo talking head with a 3D motion-graphics insert (320×618, 42 s)
- **Captions:** one phrase at a time, centred mid-frame, white, word by word. Quotes appear as "mom", "dad".
- **Cutaways:** full-screen photos of the guests he names ("Tate", "Hormoz", "Patrick Bet-David") with the name as the caption. This is proof by showing.
- **Motion-graphics peak** (22.5–25.5 s): the frame drops into a dark 3D space where value words ("intelligence", "kindness", "work ethic") fly past on cards and type sheets. It's the one big visual idea, placed mid-video to re-hook.
- **Taken:** one big graphic moment mid-video (the demo → real-world sequence) and full-screen proof flashes for named products (Nirvaha, LN Sports Arena).

## Ref 3: product ad, talking head plus UI (384×680, 25 s)
- **Split screen:** the UI or motion graphic is on top and the speaker below, then everything goes full-screen to the UI for the demo.
- **A blue colour slam** with a single word ("Yeah, hard part.") as a pattern break.
- **Captions inside the UI:** words highlighted in boxes over the transcript.
- **End:** fanned cards + logo + a comment field typing "clips" (the comment-keyword CTA).
- **Taken:**
  - the split screen (the DEMO panel over Tarun);
  - the speaker kept in a circle while the graphics go full-screen;
  - the colour slam (in One Loop copper);
  - the comment box typing **BUILD** for the CTA.

## Hook decisions
- **First second:** the crash ("Please do something!" plus a red "Deployment failed" toast and a punch-in), not the setup. Tarun's cold open.
- **1.2 s:** a black card, "He had no idea what was coming."; open loop.
- **The freeze** is the pivot from comedy to expertise. The record scratch is the audio cue that the tone changes (playful → documentary).
- **Every Tarun line gets a picture of what he says:** a dying heartbeat, the demo panel ticking, the real world crashing in, proof, then the promise. The viewer is never left on a talking head for more than about 4.7 s (the one steady shot Tarun asked for on "build for the wild").
